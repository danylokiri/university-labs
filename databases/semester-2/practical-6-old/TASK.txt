Practical Work 6 - Relational Algebra Queries

Topic: Relational algebra: formulating queries and translating them to SQL.
Goal: Gain experience in writing queries in the relational database model, become familiar with the
basic operations of relational algebra and learn to apply them to optimise queries and work with data.

Task:
1. For your variant (given in an appendix to the assignment), solve the relational-algebra problems
   and write each query in relational algebra (selection, projection, join, union, etc.), then
   express it in SQL. The queries are over the classic supplier / part / project database:
   S (suppliers: id, name, status, city), P (parts: id, name, colour, weight, city),
   J (projects: id, name, city), SPJ (supplies: supplier, part, project, quantity).
   The variant's queries:
   1. Get full information about all projects located in Dnipro.
   2. Get all "project name - project city" combinations.
   3. Get the names of all parts whose name is 5 characters long.
   4. Get the cities of suppliers whose status equals 20.
   5. Get the names of suppliers from city B.
   6. Get the names and weights of parts supplied by supplier S2 and by the supplier named Shevchenko.
   7. Get the names of parts supplied by a supplier whose status is greater than 15 and whose name
      starts with the letter "S".
   8. Get the numbers of projects that receive parts from city B or parts from a supplier from city B.
   9. Get the names of parts supplied by Shevchenko in quantities greater than 200.
2. For one of the queries of your main variant (the one used in practical works 3, 4 and 5), build a
   query to its subject area in the language of relational algebra, and also give the equivalent SQL
   query. (The subject area is the farm; for example, a query that finds the brigades that have a given
   type of machinery in a quantity greater than the average for that type over all brigades on the
   farm, listing the brigade id, name and machinery count.)
3. Add conclusions.

Notes: City B is a second city value from the sample data; the original data also contains Dnipro as
city A. SQL dialect used: MySQL.
